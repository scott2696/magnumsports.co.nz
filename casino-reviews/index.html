How to read one of our reviews

Every review on this site follows the same structure so you can compare like with like, and every number in it comes from our own testing rather than from the operator.

  • The score out of 10 is a weighted sum of six criteria, not an impression. Withdrawal speed carries 25%, NZD banking 20%, bonus terms 20%, games 15%, licensing 12%, support and mobile 8%.
  • Payout times are medians from withdrawals we requested and timed ourselves, broken out by method. Advertised figures are ignored.
  • Bonus terms come from the full terms document rather than the promotion banner. Where the two contradict each other, we say so.
  • The weekly withdrawal cap is listed on every review because it is the number most people check too late.
  • Licensing is verified against the regulator’s public register. “Not published” means exactly that, and we flag it prominently.
  • Cons are real cons. If a review has no meaningful criticism in it, it is an advertisement rather than a review.

Frequently asked questions

How do you review online casinos?

We open an account, deposit our own New Zealand dollars, play across the lobby, contact support four times including at least once in the New Zealand evening, request a withdrawal and time it. Then we score against six weighted criteria — withdrawal speed 25%, NZD banking 20%, bonus terms 20%, games 15%, licensing 12%, support and mobile 8%. The whole method is published on our methodology page.

Do you review casinos you do not recommend?

We publish reviews of operators that paid us and met our minimum standards, including ones with meaningful problems — Roby does not publish a licence number and we say so prominently. Operators that refused withdrawals, confiscated balances or could not produce a verifiable licence are excluded entirely rather than reviewed negatively. We excluded 22 operators in 2026.

How often are these reviews updated?

Every operator is re-tested at least quarterly and immediately if we receive credible reports of payment problems. Bonus terms are re-checked monthly because they change without notice. Each review carries the date it was last reviewed.

What does the score out of 10 mean?

It is the weighted sum of the six criteria above, not a general impression. A site scoring 9.1 for fast payouts and 8.1 for a large bonus with poor disclosure reflects the weighting: payout behaviour is worth 25% of the score and a bonus headline is worth nothing at all on its own.

What people ask before they open an account

Trust questions dominate the search data around casino reviews. These are the ones Kiwis type most often.

Questions sourced from Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo autosuggest for New Zealand · checked 14/09/2026

01Are online casino games legit?

At licensed operators running titles from established studios, yes — and the reason is structural rather than a matter of faith. The casino does not run the games. Pragmatic Play, Evolution, Play’n GO and the rest do, on their own servers, with random number generators audited by independent labs. An operator can no more adjust a Pragmatic Play title’s RTP than a pub can adjust a Sky broadcast.

02What is the most legit online casino in NZ?

Judge it on three checkable things rather than a badge: a licence number you can verify on the regulator’s public register, a named operating company, and a payout record someone has actually tested. On those, Spinjo (Rabidi N.V.) and Rooster Bet (Dama N.V.) rate highest here. Roby publishes neither, which is why it sits where it does.

03How do you tell if an online casino is safe?

Five minutes of checking answers it. Find the licence number in the footer and verify it against the regulator’s register — a badge image proves nothing. Confirm a named legal entity and registered address appear in the terms. Read the withdrawal ceilings and fees. Complete verification on day one. Then test with a small withdrawal in the first week, which buys you information about how the operator behaves when it owes you money.

04Are online casino apps legit?

Be careful here. Apple and Google restrict real-money gambling apps in New Zealand, so almost no offshore operator ships one to the local stores — which means an app claiming to be one deserves scrutiny. The legitimate route is the operator’s mobile web app: open the site in Safari or Chrome and use Add to Home Screen. Full-screen, app-like, no store account, nothing to install.

05Can you trust casino reviews on the internet?

Treat any review site as an interested party until it shows you otherwise, this one included. The questions worth asking: does it publish its scoring weightings? Does it say what disqualifies an operator? Does it ever criticise a site it links to? Does it disclose commission plainly? Our answers are on the methodology page, and we would rather you read it sceptically than not at all.

06Is SkyCity online legit?

SkyCity is a New Zealand-listed company operating the country’s largest land-based casinos, and it runs a licensed online casino in a jurisdiction where it holds a licence. It is a different kind of proposition from the offshore operators we review here — more local accountability, generally smaller bonuses. We do not have a commercial relationship with SkyCity and do not review it, which is also why we can say that plainly.
